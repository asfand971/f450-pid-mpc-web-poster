Model-in-the-Loop Evaluation of Cascaded PID and Linear MPC under Payload-Transition Conditions for an F450-Class Medical Delivery Quadrotor

Asfandyar Khan, Asher Gammon, and Dr. Ma Mohin
University of Greater Manchester

Abstract— The integration of Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) into autonomous medical logistics introduces critical nonlinear control challenges, specifically concerning flight stability during sudden mass and inertia shifts associated with payload delivery. While traditional continuous-hover scenarios are extensively documented, the transient dynamic disturbances resulting from instantaneous payload release remain underexplored. This study addresses this gap by evaluating and directly comparing the performance of a cascaded Proportional-Integral-Derivative (PID) baseline against a Linear Model Predictive Control (MPC) architecture within a standardized Model-in-the-Loop (MIL) framework. The results indicate significant efficiency gains in MPC regarding disturbance rejection.

I. Introduction

The deployment of multirotor UAVs for the transportation of critical medical supplies requires absolute flight reliability. Unlike standard aerial photography missions where vehicle mass remains constant, autonomous delivery involves a distinct payload-release phase. The instantaneous release of a payload drastically alters the vehicle's center of mass and moments of inertia mid-flight.

A. The Research Gap

Existing controller evaluations heavily bias toward continuous waypoint tracking. Comparisons between PID and MPC are frequently conducted on differing plant models, rendering performance deltas ambiguous. Transient dynamic disturbances resulting from instantaneous payload release remain critically underexplored. This makes it difficult to determine whether observed performance differences are caused by the controller architecture or by inconsistent experimental conditions.

B. Contributions

This paper makes the following contributions: 1) It provides a fair Model-in-the-Loop comparison of cascaded PID and Linear MPC under identical delivery mission conditions. 2) It frames payload release as a controller-critical event rather than a simple mission step. 3) It adapts the MATLAB/Simulink delivery-drone workflow toward a low-cost F450-class Pixhawk-based platform. 4) It defines a practical evaluation metric set for delivery-class controller comparison. 5) It provides a foundation for later Software-in-the-Loop (SIL) and Hardware-in-the-Loop (HIL) testing.

II. Reference Platform & Modelling

This study focuses on a low-cost F450-class quadrotor platform based on the HAWK'S WORK F450 kit, utilizing Pixhawk flight logic. The platform is suitable for research and secondary development because it includes the main components required for a functional quadrotor.

To ensure high fidelity, the mathematical plant must accurately reflect the physical UAV. A CAD assembly was developed in SolidWorks. The extracted data confirms a bare airframe mass of 1.1 kg. With a medical payload of 0.5 kg, the maximum takeoff weight (MTOW) is modeled at 1.8 kg (with safety tolerances). The actuator and thrust models utilize empirical thrust coefficients derived from 4S LiPo bench testing.

III. Control Architectures

A. Cascaded PID Control

The industry standard relies heavily on cascaded PID loops due to computational simplicity. However, classical PID control is fundamentally reactive; it calculates corrective motor commands only after an error threshold is breached. When a medical payload is dropped, the immediate reduction in required thrust causes a severe upward acceleration. PID cannot preemptively adjust; it must wait for the altitude error to accumulate before braking.

B. Linear Model Predictive Control

MPC utilizes an internal dynamic plant model to predict future vehicle states over a receding finite time horizon. By solving a constrained optimization problem, MPC can anticipate trajectory deviations and dynamically adjust control inputs while strictly adhering to empirical actuator saturation limits.

IV. MIL Evaluation Framework

The framework is constructed within MATLAB/Simulink utilizing the UAV Toolbox. Variant Subsystems allow seamless toggling between controllers without altering the physics engine. A custom script automates the mission: ascending to 15m, navigating to the target, and injecting a dynamic timeseries mass reduction (1.6kg to 1.1kg) exactly at t=25.0 seconds.

V. Conclusion

By isolating the payload drop as a specific step-disturbance, we demonstrate that MPC architectures provide superior transient recovery compared to reactive PID baselines, ensuring the safety of the medical UAV post-delivery. The completion of this MIL evaluation serves as the prerequisite baseline for subsequent hardware integration.
